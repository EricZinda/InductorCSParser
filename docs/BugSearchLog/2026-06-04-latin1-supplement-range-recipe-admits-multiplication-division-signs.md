    - Found and fixed (headline): the "Homoglyph Confusables" recipe in `docs/UnicodeGotchas.md` built its Latin-only set as `TokenSet.Ascii.Letters | TokenSet.Range(0x00C0, 0x00FF)` and the comment called that range "Latin-1 Supplement letters." The classic Latin-1 gotcha: U+00D7 MULTIPLICATION SIGN and U+00F7 DIVISION SIGN (both Sm) sit inside the U+00C0..U+00FF block between the accented-letter runs, so the range admits × and ÷ as identifier characters. A security-sensitive "Latin script only" grammar (the recipe's stated use case) would accept `a×b` and `a÷b`. Fixed the recipe in the doc and in both mirrored tests (`DocExamples/UnicodeGotchasExamples.cs`, `Core/UnexpectedUnicodeTests.cs`) to use three sub-ranges (À..Ö, Ø..ö, ø..ÿ) that skip U+00D7 and U+00F7.
