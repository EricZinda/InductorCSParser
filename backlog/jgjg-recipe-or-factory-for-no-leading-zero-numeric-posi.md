# Recipe or factory for "no-leading-zero numeric position"

    - Where this came up: the SemVer 2.0.0 sample under E2ESamples/SemVer/. The spec rule on major/minor/patch is "0 OR [1-9][0-9]*" (no leading zeros except the single zero), and the same rule applies to numeric pre-release identifiers. The natural PEG translation is `Or(Token('0'), And(OneOf(Range('1','9')), ZeroOrMore(OneOf(Digits))))` and that has a real problem: on bad input "01.2.3" the Or commits to `Token('0')` (it matched), the parser advances to offset 1, the next rule (`Token('.')`) fails because the next character is '1' not '.', and the user sees "Expected '.' after major version" at column 1 instead of "leading zero in major version" at column 0. The error message points one column past the actual problem.
    - The workaround we shipped: accept any digit run in the grammar (`OneOrMore(OneOf(TokenSet.Ascii.Digits))`) and reject leading zeros with a positioned error in the consumer, after the parse, using `result.SourceRangeOf(numericNode).Start.Column`. That works and gives the right column number, but it splits the spec rule into two layers (the grammar accepts more than the spec, the consumer narrows it back). New users coming from regex / parser-combinator backgrounds will reach for the natural PEG translation first and only realize the error-position issue after they write tests.
    - Why this matters: the no-leading-zero numeric rule is everywhere. JSON numbers, SemVer, IPv4 octets, IPv6 hextets, BCD time formats, currency amounts in lots of DSLs, RFC 3339 timestamps, MIME parameters, you name it. Every grammar that handles numbers has to think about whether to allow 007 and where the error message should point if not.
    - What to do, in increasing-effort order:
        - Recipe doc. Add a section to docs/Recipes.md (or wherever recipes live) titled "Numeric values without leading zeros" that walks through the bad PEG translation, shows the error-position trap with a concrete failure, and then shows the post-parse-validation pattern. Show the exact `SourceRangeOf` snippet so a copy-paste reader can apply it. Cost: an hour of writing.
        - Factory method. `Rules.Number(NumberFormat.Integer)` or `Rules.NoLeadingZeroInteger(name: "...")` that hides the post-parse check inside a single rule. The challenge is wiring the post-parse validation through: a normal `Rule` runs at parse time, not after, and the consumer is the only one who currently calls `SourceRangeOf`. A custom rule subclass could carry its own validator and emit a `WithError`-style positioned message at parse-record time, but plumbing that into the deepest-failure tracker is real work. Cost: half a day, plus tests, plus a decision on what other built-in numeric formats to expose (decimal-with-leading-zero allowed, hex with no leading zeros, etc.).
        - Consider whether the framework itself should track "deepest failure" position better in the `Or(short, long)` shape. The error-position principle says we record where each leaf failed, but the case here is different: the Or didn't fail, it committed to the wrong branch. Hard to fix at the framework level without breaking the PEG-commit semantics that make grammars predictable.
    - Recommend the recipe doc as the first step. It's cheap, it captures the gotcha, and the factory shape only becomes obviously right once a few more grammars have hit the same wall.
    - Done when: a copy-pasteable recipe exists for "validate a no-leading-zero numeric position with a positioned error", verified against a small grammar in the repo's test corpus.
